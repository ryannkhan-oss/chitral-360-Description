01 · Landscapes and journeys

Where the earth
touches the sky.

From the Chitral River to the high meadows of Qaqlasht and the great wall of Tirich Mir, the landscape changes with every turn.

Places worth the journey

Explore a few well-known places across Lower and Upper Chitral. Travel conditions vary by season; check locally before setting out.

Chitral Town

The district's main town, set beside the Chitral River beneath the Hindu Kush.

Kalash Valleys

Bumburet, Rumbur and Birir are known for their distinct landscapes and communities.

Qaqlasht Meadows

A high open plateau in Upper Chitral, known for wide views and spring gatherings.

Chitral Gol

A protected mountain landscape close to Chitral Town.

Chitral River

The river corridor shapes settlements, fields and routes through the valley.

Tirich Mir

The highest summit of the Hindu Kush, rising above the Upper Chitral region.

02 · People and living traditions

A place of many
voices.

Chitral is home to diverse communities and languages. Its cultural life is carried through music, poetry, craft, festivals and everyday traditions.

Culture is lived, not displayed

Learn with curiosity and respect. Communities are not museum exhibits; traditions are diverse, contemporary and shaped by the people who carry them.

Chilam Joshi

A spring festival celebrated by Kalash communities in their valleys.

Clothing & craft

Traditional clothing and craft reflect local materials, skills and identity.

Music & poetry

Music and oral expression are important parts of community gatherings.

Explore the stories behind the traditions

Ask about Khowar, Kalash heritage, festivals and the languages of the region.

03 · Mountain wildlife

Wild by nature.
Worth protecting.

The mountains and valleys around Chitral support remarkable wildlife and fragile habitats. Conservation depends on healthy ecosystems and local stewardship.

Life in the high mountains

Species and habitats vary across elevation and season. The images below are from Wikimedia Commons; conservation facts should be checked against current wildlife sources.
